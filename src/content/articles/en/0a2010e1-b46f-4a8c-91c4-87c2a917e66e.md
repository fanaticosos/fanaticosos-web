Eight months of anticipation, roster adjustments and summer training under the heat culminate in this moment. On Sunday, September 13, at **12:00 p.m. Central Time**, the Bears will visit the Carolina Panthers at Bank of America Stadium.

The preseason provided some clues, but no certainties. Chicago arrives after winning the NFC North and earning its first playoff game in 15 years; Carolina also comes off a division title and a postseason appearance. This is not a matchup between a contender and a rebuilding team: it is the first test for two franchises that now must confirm their progress.

## A week of captains, decisions and mourning

Players selected five captains for the entire season: **Caleb Williams and Joe Thuney** on offense, **Grady Jarrett and T.J. Edwards** on defense, and **Cairo Santos** on special teams.

Williams' selection formalizes something that was already evident. This is his team, and in his third year, the conversation has changed. It's no longer just about proving talent or the ability to make comebacks: now he must control games, reduce mistakes and maintain the level of an offense that finished sixth in yards in 2025.

Ben Johnson also cleared up one of the offensive line's uncertainties by confirming that **Braxton Jones will start at left tackle**. Jones will protect the blind side of Williams, accompanied by Joe Thuney, Garrett Bradbury, Jonah Jackson and Darnell Wright. The unit has important names, but the real test will be their coordination in a full game.

The preparation was accompanied by a sad news. The Bears announced the passing of **Matt Suhey**, the team's Super Bowl XX champion fullback and one of Walter Payton's great teammates. He was 68 years old. His legacy belongs to an irreplaceable era of the franchise and deserves to be remembered beyond the outcome of Sunday.

## Injury report: focus on Rome Odunze

The first injury report of the season included three Chicago players. As practices are still to come and the final report will arrive on Friday, none of these designations alone determines availability for the game.

- **Rome Odunze, receiver:** limited participation due to a calf injury.
- **Ozzy Trapilo, offensive tackle:** limited participation due to a knee issue.
- **Tyson Bagent, quarterback:** did not participate due to a back injury.

Odunze's situation is the most relevant. If he plays with any restrictions, **Luther Burden III, Colston Loveland, Cole Kmet, Kalif Raymond and the rookie Zavion Thomas** could receive more opportunities. Chicago has alternatives, although none alone replicates the combination of size, reach and ability to win contested catches that Odunze offers.

Bagent's absence won't change the initial offensive plan, but it will affect the emergency order. **Case Keenum** appears as the third quarterback and would be the backup to Caleb if Bagent couldn't suit up.

Carolina reported that the outside linebacker **Patrick Jones II** did not participate due to a back injury. The running back **Jonathon Brooks**, with a groin issue, and the defensive tackle **Bobby Brown III**, also affected by a back injury, were limited. Contrary to initial reports, **Tetairoa McMillan did not appear in the first official injury report**.

## Williams vs. Young: the comparison that won't disappear

Every Bears vs. Panthers game inevitably brings up the 2023 trade. Carolina moved up to select **Bryce Young**; Chicago turned that move into a collection of players and selections that ended up including the opportunity to take **Caleb Williams** with the first pick in 2024.

The comparison is attractive, but Sunday won't be an individual evaluation. Young comes off a season of 3,011 yards, 23 touchdowns and 11 interceptions, and Carolina built around him an offense that includes McMillan, who recorded over 1,000 yards as a rookie, and the running back Chuba Hubbard.

Williams, on the other hand, set a franchise record with 3,942 passing yards in 2025. His ability to extend plays was decisive in several comebacks, including the playoff victory over Green Bay. The next step is to make the offense function before the structure breaks down.

The quarterback who manages the first downs better and avoids third down long situations will have an advantage much more important than any narrative inherited from the Draft.

## Four keys for the Bears

### 1. Protect without making the offense conservative

The line must give Williams a stable pocket, but the plan cannot be limited to short passes out of fear of pressure. Chicago has receivers capable of attacking different areas of the field and needs to force Carolina's secondary to defend vertically.

Establishing the running game with D'Andre Swift, Kyle Monangai and Roschon Johnson would help sustain the *play-action*. However, running 'out of obligation' against loaded boxes would be a concession to the opposing defense. The offense must take what the alignment allows, not fulfill an artificial quota of runs.

### 2. Win the middle of the trenches

Carolina presents size inside with **Derrick Brown, Bobby Brown III and the rookie Lee Hunter**. Garrett Bradbury and Chicago's guards must avoid letting that rotation collapse the pocket from the center, the most uncomfortable place for any quarterback.

On the other side, the departure of Gervon Dexter forces Grady Jarrett, Kentavius Street and the young Jordan van den Berg and Jayden Loving to hold the interior rotation. It's not enough to contain the run: Chicago needs pressure that prevents Young from settling in.

### 3. Force Bryce Young to win from third and long

Dennis Allen's goal must be to take away Carolina's comfortable situations. If Hubbard and Brooks consistently produce on first downs, Young can work with quick passes and an open playbook.

To avoid that, the Bears will have to defend the running game without overcommitting the secondary. This will be especially important while Kyler Gordon and Coby Bryant remain out. Cam Lewis, Malik Muhammad II and Clark Phillips III will be under watch in the interior coverages.

### 4. Don't give away field position

The first games are usually full of penalties, communication errors and missed assignments. Chicago cannot make it easy for Carolina with handoffs or special teams penalties.

Kalif Raymond and Zavion Thomas offer speed on the returns. Their main mission, before seeking the spectacular play, will be to secure the ball and deliver favorable field positions to the offense.

## The matchup in one glance

| Factor | Bears | Panthers |
|---|---|---|
| **Quarterback** | Williams offers greater explosiveness and improvisation. | Young comes off his most consistent season. |
| **Offensive Line** | Renewed inside with Jones confirmed on the left side. | Reinforced to better protect Young. |
| **Receivers** | Depth and variety, with Odunze under watch. | McMillan is the main threat of the passing game. |
| **Defense** | Needs to replace Dexter's production and cover slot absences. | Its front seven can complicate the interior of the pocket. |
| **Special Teams** | Santos provides stability; Raymond and Thomas, return ability. | Can tilt the game if forcing Chicago to cover long fields. |

## The columnist's prediction

The Bears arrive with the most complete roster and with an offensive continuity that should make a difference. Nonetheless, playing as a visitor in Week 1, with medical doubts about Odunze and an incomplete secondary, prevents treating the game as a formality.

Chicago must win if it protects the ball, avoids letting Carolina control the clock on the ground and allows Williams to attack beyond short routes. The Panthers will have options if they turn the game into a physical battle and force the Bears to rely again on a late comeback.

**Prediction: Bears 27, Panthers 23.**

As a reference —and remembering that lines change—, Chicago appeared on September 10 as a favorite by about three points, with a total near 47.5. That describes a competitive and moderate-scoring game; not an assured victory.

## The final note

Last season brought hope back to Chicago. This season must answer a more demanding question: can the Bears turn extraordinary moments into controlled victories?

Caleb Williams has already shown he can respond under immense pressure. Now he must prove he can handle the pace, reduce mistakes and lead the team without needing to become the hero every week.

Because this season the Bears don't need another hero: **they need a quarterback**.

The first test awaits in Charlotte.
