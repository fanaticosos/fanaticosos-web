The Bears, founding members of the NFL and part of the league since 1920, needed more than a century to achieve something that now seems routine under the direction of Ben Johnson. In the franchise's history, Chicago has only played two games with at least 250 passing yards and 250 rushing yards. Both occurred with Johnson leading the offense.

In the immortal words of Dennis Green: 'The Bears are who we thought they were.' Only that version no longer lives off the defense and special teams that inspired that phrase in 2006. These Bears are an offense with almost audacious ambition, built to attack until the end and determined to back up every expectation their coach has placed on them.

Chicago waited eight months to return to an official game and needed just one afternoon to change the record books again. The 59-37 victory over the Carolina Panthers produced 96 combined points, the highest recorded in a Week 1 game in NFL history. The Bears also set a franchise record for an opening game and surpassed 50 points for the first time since 2012.

It was an extraordinary offensive display: **552 total yards, eight touchdowns, and 291 rushing yards**. Caleb Williams participated in four touchdowns, D’Andre Swift recorded three, and Kyle Monangai contributed the play that definitively broke the game.

But the score also contains a warning. Carolina produced 478 yards and kept the game tied until well into the third quarter. The offense showed contender-level play; the defense left enough work for the week leading up to the divisional matchup against Minnesota.

## Caleb Williams played with control and explosiveness

Williams finished with **269 passing yards and two touchdowns**, in addition to **65 rushing yards and two touchdowns**. He generated 334 yards, recorded a passer rating of 124.1, and, above all, seemed comfortable making decisions inside and outside the offensive structure.

His 22-yard touchdown pass to **Cole Kmet** fell between two defenders in a tight space. Before that touchdown, a scramble by Williams allowed **Rome Odunze** to make a spectacular 47-yard catch over coverage. Just before halftime, the quarterback ran 29 yards to put Chicago up 31-24.

> “That's how we want to look every week. But this doesn't happen by accident: it's built during camp, spring activities, and weekly preparation.”
>
> — **Caleb Williams**

The performance wasn't perfect. Williams was sacked on a fourth-and-one from his own 21-yard line, an aggressive decision by Ben Johnson that gave Carolina a short field and facilitated the 21-21 tie. Chicago also lost the ball on their third play, when Luther Burden III received a hit from Bobby Okereke.

The difference was the response. Williams didn't try to compensate for the errors by turning every play into a feat. He used his safety passes, escaped when space appeared, and maintained control when the game threatened to turn into chaos.

## Swift and Monangai destroyed Carolina on the ground

The statistical story of the game doesn't end with the quarterback. The Bears rushed for **291 yards and six touchdowns**, a production built by the entire offense and led by their two main running backs.

**D’Andre Swift**, just days after agreeing to a three-year, $33.75 million extension, responded with **124 rushing yards and three touchdowns in 18 carries**. He opened the game with a 32-yard run and closed it with a 28-yard touchdown that set the final score.

**Kyle Monangai** added **100 rushing yards and a touchdown in 10 attempts**. His moment came with Chicago leading 38-31: a run up the middle turned into a 61-yard scramble that changed the game's momentum.

> “It was a play that changed the momentum. When you're near the middle of the field and you get a 61-yard run, it's devastating for a defense.”
>
> — **Caleb Williams on Monangai's touchdown**

Williams pointed to that play as his favorite because it combined what Johnson wants from his offense: movement in the line, receiver blocks, and a running back attacking vertically. It wasn't just speed. It was a collective execution.

> “The story of the game ended up being the rushing game. You have to recognize the offensive line, but it was a collective effort: the tight ends and receivers also blocked with huge intensity.”
>
> — **Ben Johnson**

## From 31-31 to a 21-point avalanche

Carolina wasn't dominated from the start. The game was tied four times: 7-7, 21-21, 24-24, and 31-31. Bryce Young responded with deep passes, and the Panthers capitalized on Chicago's mistakes to stay in the game.

The break came during a seven-minute and 38-second stretch between the third and fourth quarters. The Bears scored touchdowns on three consecutive possessions:

1. A penalty for roughing the passer committed by **Derrick Brown** turned a third-and-20 into first-and-10. Swift ended the drive with a one-yard run.
2. Carolina failed a fourth down near the middle of the field. On the next play, Monangai ran 61 yards to the end zone.
3. **Tetairoa McMillan** lost the ball in Panthers territory. Williams responded with a one-yard pass to **Jahdae Walker** to extend the lead to 52-31.

Carolina's mistakes weighed heavily. The Panthers finished with 11 penalties for 84 yards, suffered two touchdowns nullified by offensive interference, and saw an interception by Bobby Brown III nullified by a holding penalty by Derrick Brown.

Chicago did what good teams should: converted the opponent's mistakes into points.

## A historic offense cannot hide 478 yards allowed

Celebrating 59 points is mandatory. Ignoring the 37 received would be irresponsible.

**Bryce Young completed 23 of 37 passes for 361 yards, three touchdowns, and an interception**. Jalen Coker tormented the secondary with eight receptions, 138 yards, and two touchdowns, including a 51-yard connection. Carolina produced 55 combined points with Chicago during the first half, matching the highest score for a half of any NFL game since 2000.

Dennis Allen's secondary continues to be in transition. Kyler Gordon and Coby Bryant remain out, Jaylon Johnson is back but still needs to regain competitive rhythm, and several important responsibilities fall on young or newly arrived players.

There were positive responses. **Malik Muhammad II** recorded an interception five seconds into the second half and showed the ability to close on the ball that drew attention during the summer. Montez Sweat recorded a sack on third down and the defense generated the turnover that preceded Monangai's long touchdown.

However, the pressure was intermittent, and the coverages gave up too many explosive plays. The defense doesn't need to match the production of an offense that just scored 59 points, but it must avoid making every game a contest to reach 40.

## Special teams also changed the game

With the score 21-21, **Tory Taylor** executed a punt with a lateral kick that Jimmy Horn Jr. couldn't control. Kaden Davis recovered the ball at the 28-yard line of Carolina, and Cairo Santos converted a 40-yard field goal.

The offense didn't capitalize on the seven points, but the play stopped the Panthers' momentum and returned the lead to Chicago. In a day dominated by offensive statistics, that field position change can easily be overlooked. It shouldn't.

## The columnist's note: a statement, not a coronation

Scoring 59 points in the first game changes the conversation around the Bears. The offensive line opened up spaces, the running back duo justified the coaching staff's confidence, and Williams combined creativity with control. For one night, Ben Johnson's offense approached the wildly ambitious standard his coach has publicly established.

But a display doesn't make anyone an MVP or deliver early Super Bowl tickets. Chicago allowed 478 yards, gave up 37 points, and needed three serious mistakes from Carolina to turn a tie into a wide victory.

Both things can be true: the offense was exceptional and the defense was concerning.

The best news is that Williams didn't need a miraculous comeback. He controlled the game when it was time to separate and allowed the rushing game to finish the work. That's precisely the growth the Bears needed to see.

The next test will be different and probably less forgiving. On Sunday, September 20, Chicago will host the **Minnesota Vikings** at Soldier Field to start their divisional schedule.

The Bears have already shown they can light up the scoreboard. Now they must prove they can also shut down the opponent.
